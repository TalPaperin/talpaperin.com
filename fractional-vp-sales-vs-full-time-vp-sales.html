Fractional VP of Sales vs a full-time VP of Sales

A full-time VP of Sales often runs $30,000 a month or more, all in, plus months of search and a quarter of ramp. A fractional VP of Sales is the same hands-on seat, part-time, typically $2,000 to $12,000 a month with no contract. For most B2B companies still proving the motion, that is the lower-risk hire.

The short answer

The role is the same. Someone has to hire, train and manage the salespeople, own the playbook, run the CRM and the pipeline, and answer for the forecast. The question is whether you need that person five days a week, on your payroll, before the motion is even proven.

A full-time VP of Sales hire is a bet on a resume and a few interviews. A fractional VP of Sales is a bet you can unwind at the end of the month. I have built and run revenue for 30-plus B2B companies on five continents. You are not guessing whether I have sat in the seat. I have.

What a full-time VP of Sales actually costs

Founders remember the salary. The bill is bigger.

A VP of Sales runs $30,000 a month or more, all in, once you add compensation, recruiter fees, equity, the months it takes to find them, and the quarter it takes to ramp. If the fit is wrong, and it often is, you fire them at month two, three or four, eat the severance, and start the cycle again. I have watched founders burn hundreds of thousands of dollars this way, with nothing to show for it but lost time.

That hire is rational when the motion is already working and you need a permanent leader to scale it. It is a poor hire when you still need someone to find what is broken, write the playbook, and prove the team can sell without the founder on every call.

What a fractional VP of Sales owns

As a fractional VP of Sales I own the sales floor, part-time:

  • Hiring, training and managing SDRs, AEs and BDs
  • The playbook, the talk tracks and the sales process
  • The CRM, the pipeline and the forecast
  • Coaching reps to quota, and replacing the ones who cannot get there
  • Day-to-day management of the team

I have recruited, trained and managed sales teams across five continents and 40-plus countries, from first reps to full floors. I make the team you have hit quota, and I build the team you do not have yet. I bill monthly. No long contract, no lock-in, no exit fines. If you do not like what I deliver, we are done at the end of the month and you owe nothing more.

Fractional VP of Sales or fractional CRO?

A VP of Sales runs the sales team and the pipeline day to day. A fractional CRO owns all of revenue, meaning sales, marketing and go-to-market, and the number itself. I do both. The right title depends on whether you need the sales floor run or the whole revenue function owned. Many companies start with a fractional VP of Sales and grow the scope into CRO.

If you are comparing a fractional CRO to hiring a VP, that is a slightly different question. I walk through it in Fractional CRO vs hiring a VP of Sales.

When the full-time hire is the right call

Hire a full-time VP of Sales when the motion is already repeatable, the forecast is honest, the founder is no longer the only closer, and you need a permanent leader five days a week. Do not hire one to invent the motion from scratch. That is how founders churn through expensive VPs.

When the fractional seat is the right call

  • You are a B2B company, including B2B SaaS, and founder-led sales has stopped scaling
  • A VP of Sales hire already failed
  • You need an operator this month, not after a two-quarter search
  • You want senior ownership without a $30,000-a-month lock-in
