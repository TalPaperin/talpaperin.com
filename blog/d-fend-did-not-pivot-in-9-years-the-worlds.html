D-Fend did not pivot in 9 years. The world's pain finally caught up to its product.

By Tal Paperin· May 10, 2026· 5 min read
B2B SalesGo-To-MarketHiring & Teams

Founded 2016. Raised $67M total. Growing 50%+ a year. What's worth noting isn't the price tag. It's the timing.

D-Fend didn't pivot. The world's pain caught up to the product they had been selling for nine years.

Most plateaued B2B companies are the inverse case.

You are selling 2023's pain

You built a real painkiller in 2022 or 2023. You found buyers who bled. You closed them. Revenue went up and to the right. Then it stopped.

You blame the funnel. You hire SDRs. You rewrite the homepage. You change the SDR script for the third time.

Here is what usually happens: your buyer's pain has shifted. Their CFO took a new line of authority. Their stack consolidated. Procurement got harder. The exact pain you solved in 2023 is now their seventh priority instead of their second.

Your product still works. Your story is selling 2023's pain to a buyer living in 2026.

Why nobody inside the company notices

Pain moves slowly and silently. No buyer sends you an email saying "this used to be our second priority and now it is our seventh." They just take longer to reply. They bring fewer people to the demo. They ask for a pilot where they used to ask for a contract.

Meanwhile everything inside your company is frozen at the moment it last worked. The pitch deck was built around the deals you won two years ago. The case studies quote customers describing a problem from that period. New reps are trained on recordings of those calls. Your best salespeople have told the old story so many times they could do it asleep, and they are good at it, which makes it harder to see that the room has stopped reacting.

So the diagnosis goes to the places a founder can see. Lead volume. Rep activity. The website. You add effort to a message that has aged, and effort behind an aged message gets you more meetings that go nowhere. If that sounds familiar, it is the same root cause I describe in hired reps but no pipeline.

How to find out if this is you

Pull the transcripts of your last five lost deals. Find the moment the buyer's tone changed. Nine times out of ten it is the same sentence, and it is not about price.

Do this yourself. Don't delegate it, and don't accept the "closed lost" reason in the CRM as a substitute. That field is filled in by a rep at the end of a bad week, and "price" or "timing" is the answer that requires the least explanation.

The pattern I see most: the founder is sure they lost on cost. The transcript shows the buyer pulled away on perceived implementation risk around minute 35 of the demo. The fix is not a discount. The fix is moving that conversation to minute 8.

Think about why that works. By minute 35 the buyer has been silently worrying about implementation for half an hour while you showed features. When the worry finally comes out, it has hardened into a conclusion. Raise it yourself at minute 8, along with how you handle it, and it becomes a question you answered instead of a doubt they took home.

When you read the transcripts, look for:

  • Questions the buyer asks that your deck never answers.
  • The point where the buyer's answers get shorter.
  • Words they use for their problem that are different from the words on your homepage.
  • A new person in the deal, often from finance or procurement, who wasn't part of your wins two years ago.

Then run the opposite exercise on your three most recent wins. Ask what those buyers said they were buying. If their reason is different from the reason on your slides, your customers have already told you what the new story is.

What to change once you know

Usually less than you fear. The product stays. What moves is the order and the emphasis.

  1. Rewrite the opening of your pitch around the pain buyers describe today, in their words from the transcripts.
  2. Bring the main objection forward into the first ten minutes, and answer it before it is asked.
  3. Replace the oldest case study with a recent one that speaks to the current pain.
  4. Retrain the team on the new talk track, and listen to calls for two weeks to make sure the old one is really gone. Old pitches survive in muscle memory.
  5. Check who signs now. If the buyer changed, your target list is aimed at the wrong title.

That is a few weeks of work, and it is the core of any honest go-to-market review. It costs far less than another quarter of SDR salaries pointed at a stale message.

"Maybe we just need to wait, like D-Fend did"

Fair question. If D-Fend held its position for nine years and got rewarded, why shouldn't you?

Because the two situations run in opposite directions. In D-Fend's case the pain was growing toward the product. Holding still was the right call, and they had raised $67M to hold still with. In the plateau case the pain is moving away from your story. Waiting only widens the gap, and most companies in that position don't have nine years of money.

There is a simple way to tell which case you are in. If buyers get more worried every year about the problem you solve, hold your ground and keep selling. If they shrug at a problem that used to make them wince, your story needs to move, and soon.

If your win rate has dropped in the last twelve months and you cannot point to the exact month it broke, that is the signal. Go find the month. The transcripts will tell you the rest.
