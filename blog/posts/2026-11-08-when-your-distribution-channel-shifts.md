HyperGuest just raised 25 million dollars from Apax, and the reason is a sentence every founder should tape to their wall: AI is changing how hotels reach travelers. The channel is shifting. Money is moving to whoever controls the new one. That story is not about hotels. It is about you.

Ask yourself one question. If the channel that brings you most of your customers changed next quarter, what happens to your revenue?

For a lot of companies the honest answer is "most of it disappears." That is not a strategy. That is a tenant who thinks they own the building.

## You do not own demand you rent

Most companies grow on one dominant channel. All the leads come from Google. Or one partner sends the deals. Or a single ad platform fills the pipeline. Or an app store is the front door. It works, it is efficient, and it feels like a strength right up until it becomes the single biggest risk on the cap table.

Because you do not own that channel. You rent it. And the landlord can raise the price, change the algorithm, deprioritize your category, or get disrupted by AI, and your revenue moves with their decision, not yours. Travel agents owned hotel distribution until the OTAs took it. The OTAs owned it until Google changed search. Now AI is changing it again. At every one of those turns, the companies that depended on the old channel got repriced overnight.

The shift is never the real problem. The dependence is.

## Build a portfolio, not a pipe

The fix is not to predict the next channel. Nobody can. The fix is to stop betting the company on any single one.

Know your concentration. If one channel drives more than half your new revenue, that is your biggest unhedged risk. Name it out loud and put a number on it.

Build a second channel before you need it. The time to develop outbound, partnerships, or a direct motion is while the main channel is still working, not after it breaks. A second channel built in a panic is always worse and always later than you need. I wrote about how to [recruit channel partners](/blog/recruit-channel-partners) precisely because it is one of the most underused ways to diversify demand.

Own the relationship, not just the transaction. When you have a direct line to your customers, email, community, account relationships, you survive a channel shift because the demand lives with you, not with the platform that happened to introduce you.

## The founders who win the shift

When a channel moves, it does not kill everyone. It kills the dependent and rewards the diversified. HyperGuest did not raise 25 million because hotel distribution is stable. They raised it because it is changing and they positioned to own the new channel instead of defend the old one.

That is a go-to-market decision, and it is exactly the work I do as a [fractional CRO](/services/fractional-cro): building a [go-to-market engine](/services/go-to-market-strategy) with more than one way to reach the buyer, so no single platform's bad day becomes your bad year. Channel diversification is not a growth-stage luxury. It is the insurance policy you buy while the premium is still cheap.

Every channel you depend on today will change. The only question is whether you built the second one before it did.
