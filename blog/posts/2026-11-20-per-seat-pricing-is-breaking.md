If you sell software by the seat, the AI agent your customer just turned on is a problem you have not priced for.

Per-seat pricing rests on a quiet assumption: one person, one login, one unit of value. Charge for the people who use the tool and revenue tracks the customer's headcount. That math held for twenty years. It is coming apart.

An agent does work that used to need a person. So a customer runs the same operation, or more of it, with fewer logins. Under per-seat pricing that shows up in exactly one place: renewal. Procurement counts the seats, sees a smaller number than last year, and cuts the bill. Your product is doing more work inside that account than it ever has, and your revenue from the account goes down.

Sit with how backwards that is. You deliver more value and get paid less for it, because you tied the price to the one thing AI is built to reduce.

The argument going around the tech press is that AI agents are quietly undermining the subscription model. That is half right. Subscriptions are fine. The exposure is the unit you subscribe by. If the unit is a human seat, you built your revenue on a headcount that your own product now helps customers shrink.

So change the unit. Price against [the value you deliver](/blog/b2b-pricing-models), not the people consuming it. Depending on the product that can be usage, workflows completed, outcomes produced, volume processed, or a platform fee for access with a meter on what the system actually does. The test is simple. When a customer gets more value from you, your revenue should rise, not sit flat while their seat count falls.

This is hard to do mid-contract, which is exactly why you start now. You do not reprice a signed customer by surprise. You reprice on new logos first, where there is no incumbent number to defend. Then you [bring the new model into renewals](/services/fractional-cro), one at a time, with a story about what the customer gets rather than how many people are logged in. The companies that wait will have the conversation anyway. It will just start with the customer saying they need half as many seats, and you negotiating from behind.

One warning. Do not swing to a meter that punishes adoption. If every action costs, customers ration usage, and rationed usage is how you teach an account to churn. The goal is alignment, not a toll booth. More value to them, more revenue to you, moving the same direction.

Per-seat pricing is not dead this quarter. But it is a model built on a count that AI is pushing down, and renewals are where that shows first. The time to fix your pricing is before the renewal where a customer does the math for you.
