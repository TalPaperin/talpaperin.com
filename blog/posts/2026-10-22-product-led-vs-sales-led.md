Every year someone declares one of these dead. PLG killed sales, then sales killed PLG, and the cycle repeats on LinkedIn forever. Ignore all of it. Product-led and sales-led are not ideologies to believe in. They are motions you choose based on who buys, what they pay, and how hard the decision is.

Pick by your buyer, not by whatever is trending this quarter.

## When product-led wins

Product-led growth means the product does the selling. People sign up, get value on their own, and upgrade without ever talking to a human. It works when three things are true.

- **Low ACV.** The price is small enough that nobody needs a committee to approve it. A person expenses it or puts it on a card.
- **Self-serve value.** A user can reach a real "aha" moment alone, fast, without onboarding calls or configuration.
- **High volume.** You have enough top-of-funnel users that a small conversion percentage still builds a real business.

If that is you, do not build an expensive sales team to sell a $30 product. The math never works. Let the product convert and keep humans out of the transaction.

## When sales-led wins

Sales-led growth means humans drive the deal. It works when the opposite is true.

- **High ACV.** The contract is big enough that a buyer expects a conversation, a business case, and a real relationship.
- **Complex or committee buying.** Multiple stakeholders, security review, procurement, a champion who has to sell internally. No product tour closes that on its own.
- **Custom fit.** The value depends on configuration, integration, or change management that a user cannot self-serve.

Try to force PLG onto a six-figure, committee-driven deal and you will get a lot of signups and no revenue. Big buyers do not swipe a card. They need to be sold.

## Most B2B need a hybrid

Here is what actually happens in the real world. Very few companies are purely one or the other. Most run a hybrid, and the skill is knowing where the line sits.

A common pattern: product-led at the bottom to capture individual users and small teams cheaply, then sales-led on top the moment a company shows expansion signals worth a human touch. Product generates the signal, sales closes the expansion. Done right, each motion feeds the other.

The failure mode is picking a motion by fashion instead of by your buyer, price, and complexity, then wondering why growth stalls. That is a [go-to-market strategy](/services/go-to-market-strategy) question, and it is worth answering deliberately before you staff a team or build a self-serve funnel around the wrong assumption.
