El Al just posted record revenue. 1.27 billion dollars. In the same report, quarterly profit fell 14 percent.

Both numbers are true. The gap between them is the whole lesson.

Revenue is the number everyone quotes. It goes on the slide, in the press release, in the all-hands where everyone claps. Profit is the number that pays rent. When the first one sets a record and the second one shrinks, you did not get more valuable. You got busier.

This is not an airline problem. It is the oldest trap in B2B, and it looks the same in a software company. Growth bought with discounts. A sales team that closes by giving margin away because nobody taught them to hold price. Cost of acquisition that climbs every quarter while the average deal stays flat. The top line goes up and to the right, and the business underneath it quietly gets weaker.

The reason it keeps happening is that the top line is easy to move. Any CEO can grow revenue tomorrow. Cut the price. Add reps faster than you can manage them. Book a big logo at terms that lose money for two years. The chart cooperates. The bank account does not.

So the question is never "did revenue grow." It is "did profitable revenue grow." Did a dollar into sales and marketing come back as more than a dollar, on a timeline you can name? Is the sales team closing on value, or on the discount? Are your best customers spending more in year two, or churning quietly while new logos paper over the hole? Those are the questions a record-revenue slide is designed to let you skip.

Most of the fix is not pricing. It is sales discipline. Reps who can defend a number, a motion that qualifies out the deals that will never be profitable, a comp plan that rewards margin instead of noise. That starts with [measuring the few numbers that actually tell you if the engine is healthy](/blog/sales-metrics-founders-should-track), then [putting someone on the hook for the number itself](/services/fractional-cro), not just the activity around it.

El Al's quarter is a record headline and a weaker business in the same breath. For a B2B company the trap is identical. Chase the top line and you can always make it move. Build the engine to grow profitable revenue, and the record on the slide will finally mean something in the bank.
