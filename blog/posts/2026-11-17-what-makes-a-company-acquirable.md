Proofpoint is paying 6.3 billion dollars for Varonis. Read the number again. Nobody writes that check for source code.

At that size, an acquirer is buying a revenue engine. A book of enterprise customers who renew. A sales motion that produces new logos every quarter without the founder in every room. Expansion inside accounts that already signed. That is what 6.3 billion buys, and the product is the thing the engine happens to sell.

Here is the uncomfortable part for most founders. When a deal like this gets written up, there is usually a quieter story next to it: the company was aging. Growth had slowed. The category moved and the sales motion did not. A buyer still pays, but the multiple is lower than it would have been three years earlier, when the same technology sat on a faster curve.

So the lesson is not about cybersecurity. It is about what a buyer actually values when they value you.

They are not paying for features. Features get copied or rebuilt. They are paying for proof that revenue is repeatable. Can you show that a dollar into sales and marketing comes back as more than a dollar, on a known timeline? Can you show that customers stay, and spend more in year two than year one? Can you show that the pipeline does not collapse the week the founder goes on vacation? Those questions sit behind the price.

Most companies cannot answer them cleanly. The revenue is real but lumpy. Two reps carry the number and nobody knows why the other six do not. Renewals are a surprise every time. The founder is still the best closer in the building, which feels like a strength and is actually a cap on the valuation, because it means the engine is one person.

If you want to be acquirable at a premium, you build the engine years before you sell. You turn the [sales motion into a system](/services/go-to-market-strategy) a stranger could run from documentation. You track net revenue retention like it is the only number that matters, because to a buyer it nearly is. You get the founder out of the critical path on deals, so the company has a value that is not tied to one person's calendar.

None of that happens in the quarter before the banker calls. It happens now, in the ordinary work of [building a sales organization that runs without you](/services/fractional-cro).

The Varonis number is a good reminder. The exit is not the event. The exit is the receipt for work you did, or did not do, on the revenue engine, long before anyone made an offer.
