Calcalist ran a piece this week with a headline that should worry every founder selling software: AI companies all say their models are the best, so here is how to tell if that is actually true.

Read that twice. A business publication felt the need to teach readers how to check vendor claims, because the claims themselves have become worthless. When everyone says "most accurate," "most secure," "best-in-class," the buyer hears the same flat noise from all of you. The word stops carrying information. It just marks you as another vendor who says what vendors say.

This is not only an AI problem. It is where most B2B pitches are right now. Your deck says you are faster, smarter, more reliable. So does the deck from the three companies your prospect saw last week. The superlative is free, so it is meaningless, and your buyer knows it.

Here is the shift. Stop selling the adjective. Sell the proof the buyer can run without you in the room.

Proof is specific and it is theirs. "We cut onboarding from six weeks to nine days for a company your size" beats "fastest onboarding on the market." A named reference they can call beats a logo wall. A pilot with a pass/fail number you both agreed on beats a promise. A short, honest answer to "where are you weak" beats a page of strengths, because it tells the buyer you are not hiding the parts that will bite them later.

The reason this matters for revenue: deals do not usually die at the demo. They die at the proposal, when the buyer has to defend the purchase to someone who was not in the room and realizes they have nothing but your adjectives to repeat. I wrote about that failure point in [why deals stall at proposal](/blog/why-deals-stall-at-proposal). Proof is what survives the hallway conversation after you leave. Claims do not.

So audit your own sales motion this week. Pull your last five lost deals and ask one question: at the moment of decision, did the buyer have something concrete they could verify, or did they have your word that you were the best? If it was your word, that is the leak. Fix it before you spend another dollar on pipeline.

Senior sales leadership is mostly the discipline of replacing claims with evidence, deal by deal, until the pitch defends itself. That is the core of how I run [a fractional CRO engagement](/services/fractional-cro): less talking about how good the product is, more building the proof that closes without the founder present.
