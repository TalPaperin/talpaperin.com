The CEO of ServiceNow went on stage and celebrated having fewer employees. Calcalist called it a gorgeous situation, quoting him. Shrinking the headcount is now a thing leaders brag about, proof that AI is doing the work people used to do.

Fine. But notice what did not shrink: the number on the sales team's quota. Revenue targets never go down to match the smaller team. So the real question behind every "we did it with fewer people" boast is simple, and most CEOs do not ask it out loud: is each rep actually closing more, or do you just have fewer reps missing the same number?

Those are opposite outcomes that look identical on an org chart.

AI does not fix this, and here is the part the vendors will not tell you. AI makes a good sales process cheaper to run and a bad one cheaper to fail. If your reps already knew which deals to work, what to say at each stage, and when to walk, a tool that drafts their emails and preps their calls is a real multiplier. If they did not, you have just bought them a faster way to chase the wrong deals and lose them with better grammar. The spreadsheet shows lower cost per rep either way. Only one of them shows more revenue.

I watched this exact confusion play out when the layoffs started earlier this year. Companies cut the sales team, kept the number, and only then discovered the number had been propped up by a few heroes and a lot of noise. I wrote about it in [why AI layoffs are exposing weak sales teams](/blog/ai-layoffs-expose-weak-sales). The cut did not cause the problem. It revealed one that was already there.

So before you cut a rep or sign an AI contract to do more with less, run the test. Take your current team, at current size, and ask whether the process is repeatable without your best person carrying it. Can a median rep follow the motion and hit target? If yes, then AI and a leaner team will compound real gains. If no, you are about to make a broken engine cheaper, not better, and the gap will show up two quarters later when the targets do not.

Getting a median rep to perform like your best one is not a tooling question. It is a management question, and it is most of what I do as [a fractional CRO building a sales team that holds](/services/sales-team-building): the process first, then the people, then the tools that make both faster. In that order, less really can do more. Out of order, less is just less.
