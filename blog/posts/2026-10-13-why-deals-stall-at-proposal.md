The deal was flying. Great calls, strong demo, they asked for a proposal. You sent it. Then silence.

You follow up. "Still reviewing internally." You follow up again. "Circling back next week." Week after week the deal sits in your pipeline at 80 percent, going nowhere, and you cannot figure out why a deal this warm went this cold.

I can tell you why. The stall did not happen at the proposal stage. It happened long before, and the proposal just made it visible. You sent a price into a deal that was never actually qualified, and a price cannot close what qualification left open.

## A proposal is not a close, it is a mirror

Founders treat the proposal as the closing move. It is not. A proposal only closes a deal that was already ready to close. If it stalls, the proposal is showing you what was missing all along.

Deals stall at proposal for reasons that were there the whole time:

- **No confirmed urgency.** They have a nice-to-have, not a this-quarter problem. Nothing forces a decision, so nothing happens.
- **No consensus.** Your champion loves it, but the committee never bought in. Now your price is being reviewed by people who were never sold.
- **No real decision process.** You do not actually know how they buy, so your proposal landed in a process you cannot see or move.
- **The economic buyer never showed up.** You priced the deal for someone who has to convince the person who signs, and that person is not convinced.

If any of those were true when you hit send, the proposal was always going to stall.

## Build consensus before you build the price

The fix is not a better follow-up email. It is doing the work before the proposal, not after.

Before a price goes out, confirm the things that actually close deals. Is there real, dated urgency? Has the economic buyer been in the room and heard the value in their own terms? Do you know the buying process, step by step, from proposal to signature? Is the committee sold, or just your champion?

If you cannot answer those, you are not ready to send a proposal. You are ready to do more qualification. This is the same discipline I bring in as a [fractional CRO](/services/fractional-cro): fix the front of the deal so the back of the deal closes itself.

## Use a mutual action plan, not a hopeful email

The single best tool for this is a mutual action plan: a shared document, built with the buyer, that lists every step from today to signed, with dates and owners on both sides. Security review, legal, procurement, sign-off, all of it, agreed together before the proposal.

It does two things. It surfaces every hidden blocker while you can still act on them, and it turns "still reviewing" into a specific, dated step you can hold them to. A deal with a mutual action plan does not stall in silence, because you both agreed what happens next. The same rigor is what I teach in [sales training](/sales-training): stop hoping the proposal closes, and build the path to close before you ever send it.
