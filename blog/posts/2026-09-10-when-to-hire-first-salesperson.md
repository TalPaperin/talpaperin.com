Most founders get the timing of their first sales hire exactly wrong. They wait too long, hit a wall, and then panic-hire the first confident closer who walks through the door. The rep fails, the founder blames the rep, and six months of runway are gone.

The mistake is thinking the trigger is a number. It is not.

## The trigger is a motion, not a milestone

Hitting a revenue milestone feels like permission to hire. It is not. Revenue tells you people bought. It does not tell you whether anyone besides you can make them buy.

The real trigger is a repeatable motion you can hand off. That means you can answer, without hand-waving: who the buyer is, what makes them act now, how you reach them, what happens at each stage, and why deals close or die. If you can only say "I just kind of feel it out on each call," you do not have a motion. You have a founder doing magic. Magic does not transfer to an employee.

Hire before the motion exists and the rep has nothing to run. They flail, you assume they are bad, and you have burned cash and morale. Hire way after the motion exists and you become the bottleneck: every deal waits on you, growth caps at your personal calendar, and you stop building the company.

## What ready actually looks like

You are ready when you can hand a new person a repeatable path, not a mystery. Concretely:

- You have closed enough deals yourself to see a pattern, not one lucky logo.
- You can write down the steps a deal moves through and roughly what percent survives each one.
- You know your win reasons and, just as important, your loss reasons.
- You are turning down or delaying deals because you personally cannot get to them. That is the bottleneck talking.

If that is you, the constraint is no longer product-market fit. It is your own hours. That is exactly when a hire creates leverage instead of chaos.

## Hire for your stage, and get help building the motion

The other half of getting this right is hiring the correct profile. Your first rep is not a polished enterprise closer who needs inbound leads and a marketing team. At this stage you need a builder: someone comfortable with ambiguity who will refine the motion, not just run a finished one.
