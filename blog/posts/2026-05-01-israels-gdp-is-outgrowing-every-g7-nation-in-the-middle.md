Israel's 2026 GDP is growing at 3.8%, outperforming every G7 nation, while operating under conditions that would paralyze most global economies.

I've watched this "economic anomaly" from the inside for decades.

The current data tells a story the headlines often miss:

- **GDP growth:** 3.8%, leading every G7 nation in 2026.
- **Unemployment:** 3.2%, lower than the US (4.3%) and the EU (6.2%).
- **Inflation:** 1.9%, while the US and Europe struggle with rising oil costs.
- **Market strength:** the Tel Aviv 35 is up 20% this year, and the shekel has gained 7% against the dollar.
- **Scale:** two of the largest cybersecurity deals in history ($32B and $25B) closed this year. Both were Israeli companies.

A country with 400+ rockets fired at it is outperforming peacetime economies. Luck doesn't explain that. Systemic decoupling does.

## What decoupling means

While most global firms "wait for the market to turn," Israeli tech is closing multi-billion dollar deals in the middle of a conflict. These companies have built revenue systems that are decoupled from external volatility.

Decoupled doesn't mean unaffected. The disruption is real, and I live in it.

It means the way a company finds, wins and keeps customers was never designed around calm. In a decoupled revenue system, customers are spread across more than one market, so one bad region doesn't stop the money. Deals run on a process that more than one person knows, so a deal doesn't freeze when someone is unavailable. And nobody on the team treats "things are crazy right now" as a reason to stop calling buyers. When disruption is the normal state, you stop writing plans that assume it away.

## The pattern I see in sales teams

In 20-plus years of building sales motions, I see the same pattern: if your system requires "perfect conditions" to close a deal, the system is fundamentally broken.

Listen to how a coupled sales team explains a bad quarter:

- "Budgets are frozen."
- "It's an election year."
- "Nobody buys in August."
- "Buyers are waiting to see what happens with AI."
- "Once the market turns, these deals will come back."

Every one of those can be true. None of them is a plan. And notice what they have in common: the cause is always outside the building, which means nobody inside has to change anything.

Here is the uncomfortable test. In the same market, in the same quarter, did any of your competitors close deals? If yes, the market was open. Your system couldn't get through it.

## How to tell if your revenue is coupled

Look at four things.

**Concentration.** If one customer, one channel or one geography produces most of your pipeline, your revenue is tied to whatever happens to that one thing.

**The forecast.** Read the notes on slipped deals. If the reasons are always about the buyer's environment ("reorg," "budget freeze," "new priorities") and never about something your team did or didn't do, you have taught your team that the weather is in charge.

**The founder.** If deals stall whenever you are travelling, fundraising or sick, the system is coupled to your calendar. That's [founder-led sales hitting its ceiling](/founder-led-sales-wont-scale).

**The reason to buy now.** Ask why a customer should sign this quarter instead of next. If the honest answer is "no reason," every outside shock becomes a free excuse to delay, and buyers will take it.

## What to do about it

Start with the last point, because it does the most work. Tie your offer to a cost the buyer is already paying every month they wait. A buyer who is losing money by standing still doesn't postpone because the news is bad. If anything, bad news makes that cost hurt more.

Second, widen the base. One more channel that produces meetings. One more segment that buys, or [a second market](/services/market-entry). It doesn't need to be big. It needs to exist before you need it.

Third, write the process down and run it on a weekly rhythm that doesn't move: pipeline review, forecast call, deal inspection, same day, same format. Rhythm is what keeps a team selling when the headlines are loud. It is boring, and it is the closest thing to armor a sales org has. If you don't have that rhythm today, the guide on [pipeline and forecasting](/sales-pipeline-and-forecasting) covers the mechanics.

Fourth, change what you accept as an explanation. When a rep says the market is slow, ask what he did differently this month because of it. If the answer is nothing, the market isn't his problem.

## "Easy for you to say. My market really did freeze."

Sometimes it does. Whole sectors stop buying, and no process will sell into a segment with no money.

A decoupled company finds out in weeks, because it watches leading indicators like meetings booked and stage conversion, and it moves effort to the segments that are still buying. A coupled company finds out two quarters later, from the bank balance, after telling the board every month that the deals were "pushed."

Conditions will never line up for you. They don't line up for a country under rocket fire either, and that country is leading the G7 in growth.

The market doesn't care about your timing, and neither do your competitors. You either own the execution, or the environment owns you.
