Salesforce, the company selling you the AI that is supposed to replace your sales reps, just said it is expanding its junior hiring. Its EMEA tech recruiting lead said the company kept investing in early-career talent, right through a stretch when most of the market was cutting it.

Now hold that against the last year and a half. SDR teams thinned out. Junior AE seats frozen. "The AI can handle top of funnel." Almost every founder I talk to has either done this or is one board meeting away from it.

Here is the cost nobody puts in the model.

The junior rep is not only a cheap set of hands on the phones. That seat is your training line. Your best closer started as someone's green SDR. Your next sales manager is a sharp junior rep right now, two years from being ready. Cut the entry layer to save money this quarter and you have not saved it. You have borrowed it from the company you will be in 2028, at an interest rate you cannot see yet.

AI really does absorb a lot of the mechanical work. List building, first-touch sequencing, call notes, the repetitive follow-ups. If that work was the whole job, cutting the role would make sense. It was never the whole job. The part that matters, reading a prospect, handling a real objection, knowing when a deal is dead, only comes from reps and at bats. You cannot hire that in at the senior level forever, because the senior level is drying up across the whole market for the same reason you are drying up yours.

So the question is not AI or juniors. It is how you build a small, sharp bench on top of AI.

The answer most founders need: keep fewer junior reps, not zero. Hand the grunt work to the tools. Push the judgment work down to those juniors earlier than you used to, because the mechanical tasks that used to fill their first year are gone. Put a senior person in charge of the number and in charge of growing that bench, so the people you keep actually get better instead of just surviving. That is the difference between a team and a staffing cost.

Salesforce can afford to play a long game here. You probably feel like you cannot. But the move that looks prudent on this quarter's spreadsheet, zero juniors, all AI, one or two senior closers carrying everything, is the move that leaves you with no pipeline-generation layer and no succession the day a senior rep quits.

If you are rebuilding the team around AI rather than firing it into a corner, that is exactly the work of [building and running a sales team](/services/sales-team-building) properly: who you keep, what you measure, who owns the number. And if you are not sure the bench you have is producing, start with [the sales metrics that actually tell you](/blog/sales-metrics-founders-should-track).

Cutting your juniors to look lean is easy. Explaining the empty bench to your board in two years is not.
