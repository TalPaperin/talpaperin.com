Most founders track too many sales numbers and understand almost none of them. The dashboard is full, the board deck is colorful, and nobody can tell you whether next quarter will hit. More metrics is not more insight. It is usually just more noise.

Here are the handful that actually matter, what each one tells you, and when it should make you do something.

## The five that predict revenue

- **Pipeline coverage.** Open pipeline divided by your target for the period. Below 3x for a normal B2B motion and you are almost certainly going to miss. This is the earliest warning you have, so watch it weekly, not at quarter end when it is too late to react.
- **Win rate.** Deals won divided by deals worked. A falling win rate tells you something broke in qualification, positioning, or fit long before it shows up in revenue. If it drops, fix the top of the funnel, do not just push reps to work more deals.
- **Sales cycle length.** How long from first meeting to closed. When it stretches, cash gets tight and forecasts lie. A creeping cycle usually means you are talking to the wrong buyer or you have no compelling reason for them to move now.
- **ACV (average contract value).** What a customer is actually worth. It sets everything downstream, how much you can spend to acquire them and which motion, PLG or sales-led, even makes sense.
- **CAC payback.** Months to earn back what you spent to acquire a customer. Under twelve months is healthy for most B2B. Past eighteen and you are buying revenue you cannot afford.

Five numbers. Together they tell you whether the machine is healthy, where it is breaking, and whether you can afford to pour more fuel in.

## The vanity metrics to ignore

Now the ones that make you feel busy and tell you nothing.

- **Raw activity counts.** Calls made, emails sent, "touches." Activity is an input, not a result. A rep can send 500 emails a week and book nothing. Measure meetings booked and deals moved, not effort spent.
- **Email opens.** Open tracking is unreliable, and an open is not intent. Nobody ever paid you for opening an email.
- **Total leads.** A big lead number with no ICP filter is a vanity trophy. Ten right-fit conversations beat a thousand tire-kickers.

If a metric cannot change a decision, stop reporting it. It is costing you attention you need for the five that matter.

## Track fewer, act faster

The point of a metric is to trigger a decision. Pipeline coverage drops, you fix generation. Win rate falls, you fix qualification. Cycle stretches, you fix targeting. If a number never makes you do anything, it is decoration.
