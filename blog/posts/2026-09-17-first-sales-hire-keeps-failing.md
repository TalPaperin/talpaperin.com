You hired a salesperson. They failed. You hired another. They are failing too. At some point the pattern should tell you something: if every rep drowns in the same pool, the problem is the pool, not the swimmers.

I get called in after the second or third failed hire, and it is almost never the rep. It is the system you dropped them into.

## You hired the wrong profile for your stage

The most common mistake is hiring a closer when you needed a builder. A polished enterprise closer is brilliant at running a machine that already exists: inbound leads, a proven pitch, marketing air cover, a playbook. Drop that person into an early-stage startup with no motion and they are useless. They have never had to invent the path, only run it.

At your stage you need a builder: someone who is comfortable with ambiguity, will get on cold calls, will figure out the objections, and will help construct the motion instead of waiting for one. Hire the wrong profile and even a genuinely great salesperson fails. That is why real [sales team building](/services/sales-team-building) starts with matching the profile to the stage, not chasing the most impressive resume.

## You gave them no playbook and no management

Two more system failures kill new reps.

First, no playbook. You handed them a product and a login and said "go sell." Everything the founder knows, who buys, why, the objections, the win reasons, stayed in the founder's head. The rep had to reinvent from zero while the clock ran. No one closes fast under those conditions.

Second, no management. Hiring a rep is not the same as managing one. A new salesperson needs deal reviews, coaching on real calls, and someone holding them to the process. Founders hire a rep precisely because they are too busy to sell, then are too busy to manage, and the rep quietly drifts. This is also why training alone does not save a bad system, a point I made in [why sales training fails](/blog/why-sales-training-fails): you cannot train your way out of a missing playbook and absent management.

## Fix the system, not the person

Before you fire another rep, ask the honest questions. Did I hire the profile my stage actually needs? Did I give them a documented motion or a mystery? Did anyone manage them, or did I just check the number every Friday and get annoyed?

If the answers are uncomfortable, the fix is not another hire. It is building the profile, the playbook, and the management around the people you already have. Fix the system and average reps hit quota. Leave it broken and you will keep burning great ones.
