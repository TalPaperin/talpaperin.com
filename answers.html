Answers for founders when revenue is stuck

The questions founders and CEOs actually search when the number is slipping: why the team is not hitting quota, why the VP of Sales left, what to tell an unhappy board, why you missed the forecast, why growth stalled after the raise. Honest answers on what is really broken, and how it gets fixed.

You missed the number

The symptoms when revenue stops doing what the plan said it would.

Why did I miss my revenue forecast?

Usually the forecast was never real. It was built on rep optimism and gut, not on a pipeline inspected stage by stage with honest exit criteria. You miss the number when nobody senior owns the forecast and challenges every deal in it. Fix the inspection and the forecast stops lying. Own the number

Why is my sales team not hitting quota?

Rarely effort. It is a leaky system: no real qualification, deals that stall with no next step, a pipeline nobody inspects, and reps improvising every call. Good people miss quota inside a broken process. Fix the system first, replace people only after. Reps but no pipeline

Why has our revenue gone flat?

Growth stalls when the motion that got you here runs out of road: the same channel, the same message, the same few reps carrying it. Flat revenue is a signal the engine needs rebuilding, not more activity poured into the old one. Rebuild the motion

Why did we miss the quarter again?

A repeated miss is a system problem, not a bad month. The pipeline coverage was never real, the forecast was wishful, or the team is working the wrong deals. The same miss every quarter is a sign no one senior owns the number end to end. Fractional CRO

Why is my pipeline drying up?

Because top of funnel stopped being fed, or what enters no longer converts. Pipeline dries up when prospecting is nobody's clear job, the ICP drifted, or marketing and sales stopped agreeing on a good lead. The fix is owning the whole funnel, not just the closing end. Fix the pipeline

The sales leadership problem

When the person who was supposed to own revenue is the problem.

Why did my VP of Sales leave?

Often because they were set up to fail: no clear number, no support, a motion that was not ready, or a founder who never fully handed over the wheel. Before you hire the next one, fix what made the seat impossible, or you will lose them too. Why VPs of sales leave

My VP of Sales isn't working out. What now?

Separate two things: is it the person, or the system they inherited? A strong VP cannot win with no playbook, no pipeline and a fuzzy ICP. Diagnose the motion first. Sometimes you fix the system and keep them, sometimes you need a different seat entirely. Why VPs of sales leave

Why can't I hire a sales leader who sticks?

Because you are hiring to fill a gap you have not defined. If the strategy, the number and the motion are not owned before the VP arrives, every hire inherits chaos and leaves. Get the engine owned and built, then hire a VP to run a system that works. Do you need a CRO yet?

Should I replace my sales leader or fix the system?

Fix the system first, almost always. Most leaders are not failing on talent, they are failing inside a broken process with no air cover. Rebuild the motion, the pipeline and the forecast, and you find out fast whether the leader was the problem or the symptom. Fix the engine

The board and your investors

What to do when the people you answer to stop being happy with revenue.

My board isn't happy with revenue. What do I tell them?

Not a story, a plan. Boards lose patience with excuses, not with honesty. Show them exactly where the revenue engine is broken, the specific fixes, and the number each one moves. A credible operator plan buys more room than any optimistic forecast. How I fix revenue

How do I explain a missed quarter to my board?

Own it plainly, then show the diagnosis and the fix. Do not blame the market or the team. Boards back founders who understand exactly why the number slipped and have a concrete plan to recover it. Vague reassurance is what actually erodes their confidence. Own the number

We raised and growth stalled. How do I get back on track?

The raise bought you the right to build an engine, not just add headcount. Growth stalls post-raise when you scale spend on a motion that was never repeatable. Lock the ICP and playbook, put a senior owner on the number, then hire against a proven system. Scaling after the raise

The founder is still the sales team

When the business cannot sell without you in the room.

Why am I, the founder, still closing every deal?

Because the motion lives in your head, not in a system anyone else can run. Founder-led sales is how you find what works; it becomes a trap when you never translate it into a playbook, hire a leader and hand it over. You become the ceiling. Founder to team

How do I get out of founder-led sales?

Write down how your best deals actually get won, hire someone to own the motion before you hire more reps, and move your deals and talk tracks onto a playbook. Reps ramp on a system, not by shadowing a founder who sells on instinct. Build the team

My founder-led sales stopped scaling. Why?

What closes deals as a founder, your conviction and relationships, does not copy-paste into reps. Scaling needs a repeatable motion, a real ICP and someone managing it daily. If every deal still needs you, the problem is no owned system, not weak reps. Fractional CRO for startups

Deals stall and slip

When the pipeline looks full but the revenue does not land.

Why are my deals stalling and not closing?

Deals stall when no one owns the next step, the champion cannot sell it internally, or you never reached the real decision-maker. The fix is hard qualification up front and multi-threading, so a deal does not die the moment one contact goes quiet. Why deals stall

Why is my sales cycle getting longer?

Usually because deals enter the pipeline before they are real, so reps spend months on buyers who were never going to sign. Tighter qualification and a mapped path to signature shorten the cycle more than any amount of chasing. Fix the motion

Why do we lose deals we should win?

Most lost deals were lost early, not at the end: single-threaded on one contact, no business case the champion could take upstairs, no urgency to act. You rarely lose on product. You lose on how the deal was run. How I run deals

My pipeline is full but nothing closes. Why?

A full pipeline of unqualified deals is just a to-do list. If coverage looks great and conversion is poor, the problem is what you let into the pipeline and how it is worked, not the volume. Inspect it honestly and most of it is not real. Fix the pipeline

Marketing, leads and go-to-market

When the top of the funnel and the close stopped working together.

Why isn't marketing generating pipeline?

Often because marketing is measured on volume, not pipeline, and sales never agreed what a good lead is. Leads pile up, sales ignores them, and the handoff leaks. Pipeline comes from one shared definition, one message, and a closed loop between the two teams. Sales and marketing as one

Why do sales and marketing blame each other?

Because they run as two departments with two scoreboards. Marketing hits lead targets, sales dismisses the output, and nobody owns the gap. Put both under one revenue number with one definition of a qualified lead and the blame has nowhere to live. Align the two

Why did our go-to-market stop working?

A go-to-market has a shelf life. The channel that worked saturates, the message gets stale, the ICP shifts as you move upmarket. It stops working when you keep running last year's motion. The fix is rebuilding around who buys now, not who bought then. Go-to-market strategy

We have leads but they don't convert. Why?

Either the leads are not your real ICP, or the sales motion cannot carry them to a close. Conversion is where a weak ICP and a weak process both show up. Fix which leads you chase and how reps work them, not just how many you generate. Fix conversion

Growth, efficiency and moving upmarket

When the business is growing, but not the way the plan needs it to.

Why has growth stalled after our Series A or B?

Because headcount is not a strategy. Post-raise, companies scale spend on a motion that was never repeatable and growth flattens. Lock the ICP and the playbook, put a senior owner on the number, and instrument the pipeline so the forecast is real before you add bodies. Scaling after the raise

Why is our customer acquisition cost rising?

Rising CAC usually means the easy buyers are gone and the motion has not evolved. You are paying more to reach the same people the same way. Sharpen the ICP, fix conversion, and open channels that fit your real buyer instead of spending harder on a tired one. Fix the motion

Why can't we break into enterprise?

Because enterprise is a different sport: longer cycles, many stakeholders, procurement and security. A motion built for mid-market stalls on a six-figure deal. You need multi-threading, a real business case and someone who has closed at that level, not just bigger logos in the CRM. Selling upmarket

Why are we growing slower than our plan?

Usually the plan assumed a motion that is not actually repeatable yet. Slower-than-plan growth is the gap between a spreadsheet and a sales engine that was never fully built. Close it by owning and rebuilding the engine, not by pushing the team harder. Build the engine

So who fixes this

When you have decided the revenue problem needs an owner, not just another hire.

My revenue is stuck. Who do I bring in to fix it?

Someone who owns the number and does the work, not an advisor with a deck or another rep. The real question is whether you need a full-time hire or a senior operator who comes in, rebuilds the engine and runs it until it works. For most stuck companies, the second. What a fractional CRO owns

Do I need a VP of Sales or someone to own the whole number?

A VP runs the team you hand them. If strategy, pipeline, marketing alignment and the forecast are not yet owned, a VP inherits chaos. When the whole revenue engine is what is broken, you need someone who owns all of it first, then hire the VP to run it. CRO vs VP of sales

Can someone fix revenue without a full-time hire?

Yes. A fractional CRO comes in senior, owns the number, rebuilds the motion, the team and the forecast, and winds down when it runs on its own. You get the ownership a stuck business needs without a $250,000 permanent hire or the months it takes to find one. When you need one
