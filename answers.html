Answers for founders and sales leaders

The questions founders, CEOs and heads of sales actually ask me about fixing revenue: what a fractional CRO is and costs, when to hire one versus a VP or an agency, outsourced sales, go-to-market, a team that stopped hitting quota, and selling into enterprise or the US. Fractional CRO: the basics

What a fractional CRO is, what they own, and how the engagement runs.

What is a fractional CRO?

A senior revenue leader who owns your sales strategy, team, pipeline, forecast and the number itself, part time, without a full-time CRO salary or a long lock-in. Not an advisor on the side. Someone in the seat who answers for the result. More on the role

What does a fractional CRO actually do?

Owns the number and the work behind it: diagnoses where deals stall, builds the playbook, hires, trains and manages reps, inspects the pipeline, fixes the forecast, and steps into the big deals. Day to day it is running the revenue engine, not handing you a deck. What I deliver

How is a fractional CRO different from a consultant?

A consultant studies the problem, hands you a deck and leaves. A fractional CRO does the work, in your pipeline and your deals, and carries the number. If it does not move, that is on me, not on a slide. Fractional CRO, explained

How long does a fractional CRO engagement last?

As long as the number needs an owner. Most run a few months to a year, billed monthly with no long contract, and wind down once your team can carry it or a full-time hire is justified. You stop when the job is done. Interim and fractional

Can a fractional CRO work remotely and part time?

Yes. The whole point is senior ownership without a full-time seat. I run the motion remotely, on-site when a deal or a team needs it, at the intensity your stage requires, from light oversight to near-full-time. How it works

When to hire, and which option

Fractional CRO, VP of sales, consultant or agency: which one your situation actually needs.

When should I hire a fractional CRO?

When revenue has stalled and nobody senior owns the number, when you need results before you can justify a $250,000 hire, or after VP hires that did not move it. If you only need more hands, hire reps. If you need someone to own strategy, team and forecast, that is a fractional CRO. Do you need one yet?

Do I need a fractional CRO or a VP of sales?

A VP of sales runs the team you tell them to run. A fractional CRO decides what the team and the motion should be, then runs it. Early or stalled, you usually need the strategy owned first. Hire the VP once the motion is proven. CRO vs VP of sales

Should I hire a fractional CRO or a sales agency?

An agency runs activity and bills for it: emails, lists, calls. A fractional CRO owns the outcome: strategy, team, pipeline and the number. If you want more top of funnel, an agency can help. If the engine itself is broken, activity will not fix it. CRO vs outsourced sales

Should I hire a fractional CRO or a sales consultant?

Same work, different accountability. A consultant advises and leaves. A fractional CRO advises and then runs it, carrying the number to the result. If you already know what to do and just need hands and ownership, skip the deck and hire the operator. What a fractional CRO owns

When should a startup hire its first sales leader?

When founder-led sales has found repeatable wins but the founder is now the bottleneck, or when you are hiring reps with no one to build the playbook or manage them. A leader first, reps second. Reps with no system just burn runway. Fractional CRO for startups

Cost, pricing and ROI

What fractional revenue leadership costs, how it is billed, and how to think about the return.

How much does a fractional CRO cost?

$2,000 to $12,000 a month, billed monthly with no long contract. The range tracks how much of me you need, from a few hours a week of oversight to near-full-time ownership of the number. A full-time CRO runs $250,000 and up before bonus and equity. Full cost breakdown

Fractional CRO cost versus a full-time CRO salary?

A full-time CRO is $250,000-plus in base, plus bonus, equity and the months it takes to hire one. A fractional CRO gives you the same seniority in the seat for $2,000 to $12,000 a month, starting now, and you stop when the job is done. Cost compared

What is the ROI of a fractional CRO?

You are buying a fix for a stalled number, not hours. Unstick a few deals, shorten the cycle or raise close rates, and it pays for itself fast. The honest test: if revenue is not your constraint, you do not need one. The ROI case

Do fractional CROs work on commission?

No. I own your strategy, team, pipeline and forecast on a flat monthly retainer. Commission-only aligns someone to their own payout, not to building a sales engine that lasts. I am not a lead agency or a rep for hire. How pricing works

How much should I budget for fractional sales leadership?

Start from the problem. Light oversight sits near $2,000 a month; owning the number, the team and the forecast runs toward $12,000, and most land in the middle. If you also need SDRs or marketing, that is budgeted on top as a full managed team. See pricing

Outsourced sales and SDR teams

When to outsource the sales team instead of building it, and what it costs.

Should I outsource my SDR team or hire in-house?

Outsource when building is too slow, too costly or too risky for your stage: you get a working team in weeks, not six to twelve months, with no severance risk. Build in-house when sales is your core advantage and you can afford the ramp. Outsourced SDR team

What do I get with an outsourced sales team?

A full team outside your headcount: native-speaking SDRs and AEs, senior leadership and a VP, hired, trained, managed and reported on. Run by KSW Solutions, led by me. A working engine, not a pile of contractors. Outsourced sales

Why do outsourced sales teams fail?

When they are handed a list and a quota with no owned strategy, no real ICP and no feedback loop, they dial into the void. Ours works because the same person who owns your number owns the team. Activity without ownership is the failure mode. How we run it

What is pay-per-meeting and how is it different?

You pay for qualified meetings booked on your calendar, not emails or lists. We define the ICP, find and contact the right people, and book the meeting; you run and close it. A retainer bills you either way. This bills by result. Pay-per-meeting

Go-to-market that actually works

Building, fixing and changing a B2B go-to-market motion.

Why is my go-to-market not generating pipeline?

Usually the motion is improvised: a fuzzy ICP, a message marketing invented that sales does not use, and channels chosen by habit. Pipeline comes from a sharp ICP, one message both teams run, and the two or three channels that actually reach your buyer. Go-to-market strategy

What does a go-to-market engagement include?

ICP, positioning, pricing, the channels that fit, a playbook and a real forecast, then I help you run it until there is pipeline, not just slides. For SaaS, hardware, services or complex B2B. The plan is worthless if no one executes it. What GTM includes

How do I define my ideal customer profile?

Start from who already buys fastest, stays longest and pays the most, then find what they share: stage, pain, trigger, who signs. A good ICP is narrow enough to say no. If it describes half the market, it is not an ICP. GTM and ICP

Product-led or sales-led: which motion is right for me?

Match it to deal size and buyer. Low price, self-serve value and a fast first win lean product-led. Higher price, multiple stakeholders and real evaluation need sales. Most B2B companies run a blend; the mistake is forcing one because it is fashionable. Choosing the motion

Fixing a stalled sales team

When the team is in place but the number is not moving.

Why is my sales team not hitting quota?

Usually not effort. It is a leaky process: no qualification, deals that stall with no next step, a pipeline nobody inspects, and reps improvising every call. Fix the system and the same people start closing. Replace people only after the system is sound. Reps but no pipeline

I hired reps but have no pipeline. What now?

Reps are closers, not a strategy. With no ICP, no playbook and no one managing the motion, good reps starve. Put an owner on the number, build the playbook and the pipeline, then the hires you already made start to pay off. Fix the pipeline

Why are my deals stalling and not closing?

Deals stall when no one owns the next step, the champion cannot sell it internally, or you never reached the real decision-maker. The fix is qualification up front and multi-threading the deal, so it does not die the moment one contact goes quiet. Why deals stall

How do I build a repeatable sales process?

Write down how your best deals actually get won, turn that into stages with clear exit criteria, and inspect every deal against them. Repeatable means a new rep can run it without you. If it lives in one person's head, it is not a process. Build the engine

Startups and founder-led sales

Getting from founder-led selling to a team that can carry it.

When should a founder stop doing sales themselves?

Not until you have repeatable wins to hand over. Founder-led sales is how you find the motion. Hand it off too early and you scale something that does not work yet. Too late and you become the ceiling. The signal is repeatability. Fractional CRO for startups

How do I move from founder-led sales to a sales team?

Document what you do that wins, hire a leader to own it before you hire reps, and transfer your deals and talk tracks onto a playbook. Reps ramp on a system, not by shadowing a founder who sells on instinct. Build the team

How do startups get their first B2B customers?

Founder-led, narrow and direct: pick a tight ICP, reach the real buyer, and sell the outcome, not the feature list. Early customers come from focused outreach and your network, not from scaling spend before the motion is proven. Early-stage sales

How do I scale sales after a Series A?

The round buys you the right to build an engine, not just add headcount. Lock the ICP and playbook, put an owner on the number, hire against a proven motion, and instrument the pipeline so the forecast is real. Headcount without a system burns the round. Scaling after the raise

Enterprise and international

Selling upmarket and expanding into new countries, including the US.

How do I sell to enterprise as a startup?

Treat it as a different sport: longer cycles, many stakeholders, procurement and security. Multi-thread from the start, map the real decision-makers, and sell business value, not features. One champion and one demo will not carry a six-figure deal. Market entry

How do I sell into the US market from abroad?

Do not just point your existing outreach at a bigger market. US buyers expect a local message, local proof and someone in their time zone. Pick the beachhead segment, adapt the motion, and build credibility before you scale spend. Selling into the US

How do I expand B2B sales into new countries?

Pick the market on real pain, access and willingness to pay, not on where you happen to have a contact. Then build only there: the right channel or direct team, local proof, and a plan to win one market before spreading thin across five. International market entry

How do I shorten a long enterprise sales cycle?

You rarely shorten it by pushing. You shorten it by qualifying hard early, multi-threading so it does not stall on one person, and creating a reason to act now. Most cycles drag because no one mapped the path to signature up front. Complex deals

Sales and marketing as one engine

Making marketing feed pipeline that sales can actually close.

Can you run sales and marketing as one function?

Yes, and that is the point. Most companies run them as two departments that blame each other. I run them as one revenue engine, aligned to a single definition of a good lead, one message and one forecast, so marketing's leads are leads sales can close. Sales and marketing

How is a fractional CMO different from a marketing agency?

An agency runs campaigns and bills for activity. A fractional CMO owns the marketing function, its priorities, budget and people, and answers for whether it produces pipeline. One is hands you rent. The other is a leader who carries a number. Fractional CMO

My marketing makes leads but sales says they are junk. Why?

Because the two teams never agreed what a good lead is. Marketing hits a volume target, sales ignores the output, and the handoff leaks. Fix it with one shared definition, one message and a closed loop, so both answer to the same pipeline. Close the gap
