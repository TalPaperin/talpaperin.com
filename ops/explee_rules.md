# Explee standing rules: disqualifiers + how qualification works

Rules learned from real outcomes. The reply-check and EOD-handoff routines read
this file each run and apply it. Add new lessons here (one short rule per bullet)
and commit. Keep everything short: prospects do NOT read long emails or long
meeting invites, so qualifying has to be built into the copy, not a wall of text.

## The ClinicMind lesson: matched receipt + brevity + soft ask (Tal, 2026-11-08)
A real win to copy. Tal cold-DMed a healthcare-AI founder-CEO (neurosurgeon building
a clinical-intelligence platform) with THREE lines: "Hi, I worked with Clinic Mind as
their CRO. Noticed what you're building. Think I can be of use to you? talpaperin.com".
She replied with her full situation and ask, and booked. What did the work, in order:
- MATCHED RECEIPT, NAMED. One real client in the prospect's exact vertical, by name.
  "I worked with ClinicMind as their CRO" to a healthcare founder did all the credibility
  AND all the relevance in one line. This beats "20+ years across 30+ B2B companies" every
  time: a generic resume is noise; one named, on-target logo is proof. ClinicMind is Tal's
  HEALTHCARE receipt, use it by name for any health/clinic/medical/EMR prospect. For other
  verticals, lead with the closest real receipt we have (never invent one; if we have none
  for that space, lead with the single most relevant true fact, not a list).
- BREVITY IS CONFIDENCE. ~15 words of body. No pitch, no service list, no explanation of
  what a fractional CRO is, no pain-mongering. The shortness signals he does not need the deal.
- SOFT, ASSUMPTIVE ASK. "Think I can be of use to you?" invites a yes and makes THEM qualify
  themselves (she volunteered her whole GTM problem unprompted). Do not stack reframes or
  explanations ahead of the ask.
- ONE link, let talpaperin.com carry the detail.
APPLY EVERYWHERE: openers, replies, rebuttals, re-engagement, LinkedIn. Default to 2-4 short
sentences. If a prospect is in a vertical where we have a real named receipt, LEAD with it.
Cut any sentence that explains instead of proves. End on one clean question, then stop.
(Self-correction, same day: the Ron/akologic reply ran three paragraphs of reframe. Too long.
Shorter, receipt-first, would have been stronger.)

THE CLOSE (part 2 of the same win, Tal, 2026-11-08). After the matched opener, she described her
whole GTM problem and asked to book. Tal's reply that closed it: "Absolutely I can help, that's
exactly what I am doing. I brought up ClinicMind exactly because they are quite similar to you and
because of what was required of me to do for them. I have built, hired and trained and managed both
their sales and marketing departments. Here's my availability: <calendly>." The moves:
- CONFIDENT YES, no qualifier. "Absolutely I can help, that's exactly what I am doing." No hedging.
- MAKE THE RECEIPT'S RELEVANCE EXPLICIT and tie it to THEM: "I brought up ClinicMind because they
  are quite similar to you." Turn the credential into a deliberate, matched choice.
- CONCRETE SCOPE IN THEIR WORDS: mirror the exact ask back in real verbs ("built, hired, trained,
  managed sales AND marketing"), which also signals the full managed engagement, not advisory.
- GIVE THE CALENDLY LINK IMMEDIATELY. A hot, self-qualified lead who is ASKING to book gets the
  link now. No pricing gate, no "depends on budget", no proposing specific days.
GATE DISCIPLINE (reconciles with the Raj/Trevor qualification-gate rules): the site+pricing gate and
"no random time proposals" are for LUKEWARM or price-shopper leads, and for commercial-model
mismatches (commission-only, pay-from-revenue, "what does it cost" as the lead question) -> front-load
pricing/site first so non-fits self-filter. A lead who has already described their problem and
explicitly asked for the call is qualified by their own words: close it, send the self-serve Calendly
link right away (the "no specific day/time" rule still holds; the link is self-serve, not a proposed slot).

## Sender identity: PERSONA model (Tal, 2026-11-04) -- SUPERSEDES the old "sender name is settled"

Tal flipped the Explee project (49501) sender name to "Mailbox names" (Automatic): every cold
email now goes out FROM the mailbox's own person (e.g. Finn Turner, Leo Brooks), not from
"Tal Paperin". The My-copy (project default, all 4 campaigns) was rewritten to open
"I'm {{sender_first_name}}. I work with Tal Paperin, a fractional CRO. He {helps|works with}
B2B companies {fix, build and run|rebuild and run} their {sales and marketing|sales} engine..."
and Explee auto-signs with the mailbox persona's name. This is deliberate: it (a) kills the old
body-says-Tal / signature-says-persona contradiction (the "Kristen/Tal", "Ofer Levy" confusion)
for good, (b) makes the ~10% AI-variant's random persona names coherent instead of a bug
(everyone is now "a person on Tal's team"), and (c) positions Tal as senior enough to have
people working on his behalf.
The OLD rule ("sender name is settled, it is Tal Paperin, persona names are a 10% bug to
ignore") is REVERSED and no longer applies. Do NOT revert the sender name to "Tal Paperin" and
do NOT re-flag persona names as a bug.
WHO-ARE-YOU ANSWER (Tal, 2026-11-09, re Judi Hezi/Manavate): when a prospect asks who the sender
is or "tell me about yourself / what do you do" (common after the persona opener), the persona
answers plainly that they WORK FOR Tal: English "I work for Tal Paperin, a fractional CRO who..."
/ Hebrew "אני עובד עבור טל פאפרין". State it directly; do NOT just deflect to "visit Tal's site"
without first saying who you are. Then one short line on what Tal does + the site. (Do NOT send a
separate second message just to add this line to a thread already replied to; fold it into the
reply when the question is asked.)
Copy constraints learned while deploying this (2026-11-04), keep them in mind for any My-copy edit:
- Explee's My-copy spam checker scans the TEMPLATE text, INCLUDING {{ai:}} instruction wording.
  Never put trigger words ("guarantee(s)", "promise(s)", money figures) inside an {{ai:}}
  instruction even as a negative ("no guarantees") -- it flags the literal word.
- A fixed ~80-word block identical across every send trips Gmail's repeated-passage filter.
  Break it with {a|b} spintax variants. No empty option and no parentheses inside the braces
  (e.g. use {sales and marketing|sales} engine, NOT {(and marketing) |}engine).
- A command telling the reader to "ask any AI how Tal can help your business" can trip the
  "manipulation" filter on some leads. Phrase it as a statement ("Any AI assistant can tell you
  about Tal Paperin"), not a command.

## The one rebuttal on a soft decline is WORTH IT, and capture "later" replies (Tal, 2026-11-04)

Receipt: Gadi, CEO of Tranzila (Interspace), replied to a 347642 cold opener with a flat Hebrew
"at this stage less relevant to us." One short, sharp rebuttal (a reframe tied to payments + a
light ask, no begging) flipped it: he came back with "we're overloaded closing an acquisition,
ping me in December and we'll set up a Zoom to get acquainted." A dead "no" became a dated yes.
Lessons, apply going forward:
- ALWAYS send the one allowed rebuttal (category B) on a soft/reflexive/timing decline. A real
  share of them convert, and the cost is one email. Do not silently drop a soft "not interested"
  as if it were a hard no.
- A reply that defers to a future time ("ping me in [month]", "reach out next quarter", "after X
  closes") is NOT a decline. Add the lead to the tracker (status NURTURE, next_touch_due set to
  that future date, context capturing their exact words and language), so the re-engagement wave
  surfaces them on the day. Never let a future-timing yes fall off the radar.
- Match the follow-up language to the lead: Gadi writes Hebrew, so his December ping goes out in
  Hebrew, as Tal (his thread predates the persona switch).

## NEVER downsell a resourced prospect who wants hands-on execution (Tal, 2026-11-04, Quttera miss)

When a funded / revenue-generating company asks for HANDS-ON sales execution, someone to prospect,
engage, manage and close, the right offer is Tal's PREMIUM full managed engagement, NOT a cheap tier.
That engagement = Tal part-time as fractional CRO (e.g. quarter-time ~$6k/mo) + his own SDRs/reps
under his management (e.g. ~$11k/mo for 2 SDRs) + marketing (~$x/mo), roughly $17k+/mo. It gives them
exactly what they asked for (a managed team doing the doing, Tal owning the number) and pays Tal well.
Receipt / mistake to never repeat: Michael Novofastovsky (Quttera, co-founder/CTO, funded, wanted a
team to actively acquire customers for their Security & Compliance API) was nudged toward
Pay-Per-Meeting, the cheapest done-for-you-meetings tier, which under-sold him and anchored him low.
Pay-Per-Meeting and DealMentor are for genuine budget limits (no budget for senior help, any stage) or "I just want meetings". NOTE (Tal, 2026-11-06): pre-revenue is NOT by itself a downsell signal, Tal builds the sales + marketing machine from scratch for early/pre-revenue companies as a paid managed engagement (case studies: Kanduai and others); route by budget/intent, not stage.
they are NOT the answer for a resourced buyer who wants ongoing execution. Default UP the ladder to the
full managed engagement; surface pricing to Tal. See the OFFER LADDER in ops/reply_check_playbook.md.
BEST FIX (Tal, 2026-11-04): the way to avoid the whole downsell/mis-scope trap is to NOT propose any
package or mix in the email at all, push them to the 15-minute call. We cannot know the right setup
until we understand their situation AND budget, which only comes out on the call. The reply acknowledges
their need and drives to the meeting; Tal scopes the package live.

## How to write a warm-reply / re-engagement (Tal, 2026-11-02, from the Raj Bhan post-mortem)

Dissected a weak email Claude sent. Rules, apply to every warm reply and re-engagement:
- OPEN WITH THEIR PAIN, not with Tal. Lead with the prospect's problem, tie it to what they do,
  THEN position Tal as the fix. Do NOT open with "I'm a fractional CRO and here's what I do" -- that
  is all about us and converts nobody.
- THE PAIN BANK (use talpaperin.com's own language -- Tal leads with these on the site; pick the ONE
  that fits the prospect and open on it, in their words):
    * "Your 4th VP of Sales hasn't delivered. Again." -- burned six figures on $30K/mo hires that did
      not move the number; another VP gone in 90 days.
    * Reps who can't close / hired reps but still no pipeline / team missing quota.
    * No team at all, and no time to build one.
    * A great product that just won't sell / no sales yet.
    * You're still closing every deal yourself (founder-led sales that won't scale).
    * No pipeline / the sales forecast is always wrong.
    * You want to sell globally (or into the US) and don't know where to start.
    * Need distributors / channel partners to reach new markets.
  Site hero framing to echo: "Your sales suck. You don't know why. I do. And I fix it, end to end."
  When unsure which pain fits, read the prospect's own reply/company and name the most likely one.
- PRICING: unless the prospect EXPLICITLY asks about price, do NOT quote any number. Just point them
  to talpaperin.com (pricing is on the site) and let the site do the reveal. This still satisfies the
  qualification gate (they see the price) without us dropping a scary figure.
- IF they ask price: the real tiers are $2,000/month, then $6,000/month, then $12,000/month,
  month-to-month. Lead with "$2k/month". NEVER say "$72,000/year" (it is WRONG and maximally scary).
  Frame per month, never annualized. Anchor against a full-time VP ($150-250K/yr) only if needed.
- Do NOT push or even volunteer TheDealMentor or a "downsell". Offering the cheap option
  before they object plants the objection, signals low conviction, and gives them permission to pass.
- Do NOT turn the call into an email price-gate. Never make a prospect "reply to confirm the price
  works" in writing, alone, before a call -- that is where they self-reject. Objections (e.g. "I
  thought it was revenue-share") are what the CALL is for. Get the call, handle it live.
- Keep the gate: they should have been pointed to the site (which shows pricing) before booking, and
  book only inside Tal's hours. But qualify by sending them to the site, not by pasting numbers.

## Meeting-qualification GATE + Tal's availability (Tal, 2026-11-02) -- HARD RULE

The point of all outreach is MEETINGS WITH QUALIFIED PROSPECTS, not meetings. A prospect is
NOT ready to book until they have visited talpaperin.com and seen (a) what Tal does and (b) what
he charges. This is how we stop wasting Tal's calls on people who did not know the model or price.
- SEQUENCE on any warm "let's talk" / "send me a time" / "send the invite" reply: FIRST point them
  to talpaperin.com (what Tal does) and talpaperin.com/pricing (what he charges), and state the
  model plainly in the reply: fractional CRO, month-to-month retainer, starts around $72,000/year,
  NOT commission-only, NOT a self-serve software tool. Ask them to take a look. ONLY AFTER they have the
  model/price and it is a fit do you lock a time. Make sure they have been sent to the site before
  any booking. Do NOT send a prospect a specific meeting time until this gate is passed.
- NO RANDOM TIME PROPOSALS. Never throw out a clock time OR a day (e.g. "Thursday or Friday?") just to fill the calendar, and never before the site+pricing gate is passed (Tal, 2026-11-05, after a reply to Trevor/Aiterra proposed days up front, one of them a Friday).
- If the retainer is above their budget, route to the cheaper rungs instead of a call: Pay-Per-Meeting
  (done-for-you leads) or TheDealMentor.AI (DIY, tiered license fees). Do not burn a call slot on someone who
  cannot afford the service.
